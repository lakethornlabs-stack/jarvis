JARVIS privacy policy

Last updated: 8 November 2026

JARVIS is a personal assistant that runs entirely on its owner's own computer. It is used only by its owner, who is also its developer. This policy describes how JARVIS handles Google user data.

What JARVIS accesses

When the owner connects their Google account, JARVIS can access:

  • Gmail: reading, searching, labelling, archiving and trashing messages, and creating drafts.
  • Google Calendar: reading and managing events.
  • Google Drive: reading, creating, trashing and sharing files.
  • Google Tasks: reading and managing tasks.
  • Google Contacts: read-only lookup of names and email addresses.
  • Basic profile: the account's email address and first name.

How the data is used

Google data is used only to carry out what the owner asks for, such as answering "do I have unread email?" or preparing a draft. JARVIS never sends email, invites guests, replies to invitations or shares files without the owner reviewing and approving each action. It cannot make purchases or payments.

Where the data goes

  • Data is processed on the owner's computer by a local language model. The sign-in token is stored on that computer and can be read only by the owner's user account.
  • JARVIS keeps a local log of the actions it takes, such as "created a draft to …", on the owner's computer.
  • If the owner chooses to ask the optional "Claude" persona about their Google data, the relevant content is sent to Anthropic's Claude service under the owner's own account to answer that request.
  • Google data is not sold, not used for advertising, not used to train AI models, and not shared with anyone else.

JARVIS's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing access and data

The owner can disconnect Google at any time in JARVIS's settings, which revokes access at Google and deletes the stored token. Access can also be removed at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Local logs can be deleted from the owner's computer at any time.
